METC 2025-2026 Semester 1 Physics Course Syllabus - Light


This course is expected to have a total of 8 lessons, and will systematically explain the knowledge of "light" in physics to students. Including the concept of light, common behaviors of light, and applications in optical instruments;


In each class, the class will be taught in the form of knowledge point explanation + experiment (including invisible light demonstration, imaging, refractive reflection, etc.)/activity/handicraft (including kaleidoscopes, telescopes, etc.) + question and answer; The course will end with a final output.


The curriculum vision
leads children to explore the secrets of light with their own hands, understand its properties and wonderful phenomena, and make their own optical works with their own hands, so that scientific knowledge can be turned into visible results.


Children's curiosity Elementary school students often ask:

  • "Why does the sun turn into a rainbow when it hits the water?"

  • "How does the mirror reflect my appearance?"

  • "Why can't some light be seen, but you can take pictures?"

  • "Why can the telescope see so far?" The answers to these questions are hidden in the world of light!


Course Highlights:

  • Intuitive demonstration: Use invisible light, refraction, reflection, imaging and other experiments to let children "witness" the characteristics of light with their own eyes.

  • Hands-on creation: Make your own kaleidoscope and improvised telescope to feel the combination of science and creativity.

  • Diverse classroom formats: explanation + experiment + manual + question and answer to ensure that every child can use their hands, brains, and mouths.

  • Presentation: At the end of the course, students will complete and present their optical works.


Expected harvest

  • Master the basic concepts of light and the principles of common phenomena

  • Cultivate scientific inquiry and experimental hands-on ability

  • Stimulate lasting curiosity and interest in learning

  • Connecting scientific knowledge to everyday life




Course Structure:




Part 1 Basic Concepts of Light (2 lessons)


Lesson 1 Basic properties of waves

Knowledge points:

  • Understand the basic properties of waves (e.g., wavelength, amplitude, frequency)

  • Light is an electromagnetic wave

  • velocity of light

  • The propagation mode of the wave

Activity:

Teachers or classmates are divided into groups to demonstrate the propagation of waves with a rope


Lesson 2 Spectrum

Knowledge points:

  • Introduce the spectrum, the application of electromagnetic waves of different wavelengths

(Radio, Microwave, Infrared, Visible, Ultraviolet, X-ray, γ-ray)

Activity:

Let students give examples of different waves/electromagnetic waves in daily life

Invisible light presentation:

  • Turn on your digital camera or phone camera and point it at the emitter of the IR remote.

  • Press any button on the remote control while observing the camera screen.

  • You'll see a flashing spot on the camera screen on the remote control's emitting end

(The sensors of digital cameras and mobile phone cameras are sensitive to infrared light.) When the remote emits infrared light, the camera can detect this light and convert it into points of light visible on the screen)





Part 2 Reflection, Refraction, Diffraction and Scattering (3 hours)


Lesson 3 Reflex

Knowledge points:

  • Understand incident rays, reflected rays, and normals

  • Know the angle of incidence = reflection angle

  • Resolve planar and diffuse reflections

  • Animated presentation

Activity:

  1. Draw a target on the blackboard and prepare a small mirror for the students. The teacher uses the laser pointer to shoot the laser onto the student's mirror, and the student has to adjust the mirror by himself, change the angle of incidence, and reflect the laser to the target of the blackboard. In groups of 3-4 people, the group competes to see which group takes the shortest time.

  2. In groups of 4-6 people, prepare three long mirrors, hard cardboard, tape, and colored scraps of paper for each group. Let students make their own kaleidoscope under the guidance of the teacher.



Lesson 4 Reflex

Knowledge points:

  • When the angle of incidence is 0, the propagation direction does not change

  • The speed of light propagation in different media is different, so the direction of propagation is changed

  • Increased velocity – farther from the normal, decreased velocity – closer to the normal

  • Animated presentation

Activity:

Place the coins on the bottom of a dry, empty bowl and ask students to step back until they can't see the coins just right. Another student pours water into the bowl, and the student who was originally unable to see the coin will see the coin again. This is because when adding water, light is refracted in the water, changing direction and entering the observer's eye. When there is no water, the light travels in a straight line and cannot enter the observer's eye at an angle.



Lesson 5 Diffraction & Dispersion

Diffraction: (Focus on dispersion and triangular prism experiments)

Knowledge points:

  • Definition: The phenomenon of bending and scattering light passing through a finitely sized hole or bypassing the edge of an object.

  • The smaller the hole, the more obvious the diffraction phenomenon (simplified version explanation, considering that elementary school students should not know what wavelength is)

Activity:

Video demonstrating the diffraction phenomenon of light and water waves.


Dispersion: (Focus on dispersion and triangular prism experiments)

Knowledge points:

  • Definition: After white light enters the triangular prism, the refractive angles of light of different wavelengths are different, thus separating into the "seven-color spectrum"

  • Introduction Sunlight is composed of infrared rays, seven-color light, and ultraviolet rays


Activity:

The strong light shines on the prism and observes the rainbow light band on a white paper








Part 3 Imaging of Light (estimated 3 lessons)


*Minimize complex terminology and change to plain language


Lesson 6 Imaging principles of light

Knowledge points:

  • The concept of real image and virtual image

  • Principles of real and virtual image imaging

  • Principles and differences between planar mirror imaging and small hole imaging


Activity:

The teacher explained the difference between real and virtual images by demonstrating small hole imaging and flat mirror imaging respectively



Lesson 7 Convex lens

Knowledge points:

  • - Definition of focal length, spindle and main focus

  • - Light paths and how images are formed

  • - Life use of convex lenses (farsightedness, magnifying glasses)

  • Understand the role of convex lenses

  • - Understand the basic phenomena of magnified and inverted imaging

  • - Know where the image is in relation to the distance of the object


Activity:

Teachers use convex lens imaging to teach

- Materials: convex lens, white paper, candle/bulb

-Operation: Move the object away and move the white paper until a clear inversion image appears

- Question leads: "Where do the images appear?" "Why is it poured?" "What happens when you get close?"



Lesson 8 Concave lens

Knowledge points:

  • Recognize that concave lenses do not magnify but make objects appear smaller

  • Understand that concave lenses cannot be imaged on paper, they are virtual images

  • The path of light and how the image is formed

  • Life use of concave lenses (myopia)

  • The difference between convex lenses and concave lenses (real and virtual images, upright and upside down, magnification and reduction)

Activity:

  1. Contrasting convex and concave lenses

Prepare a set of daily necessities (camera lenses, telescopes, etc.) for students to sort

  1. Make a telescope

Assemble a simple model using a plastic convex lens/concave lens to demonstrate the difference between near and far
