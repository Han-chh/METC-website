METC 2025-2026 Semester 1 Economics Course Outline - Basic Microeconomics


This course is expected to have a total of 8~9 class hours, which will systematically explain the basic knowledge of the "microeconomics" part of economics. including basic economic issues, market and supply and demand relations, and microeconomics;


In each lesson, knowledge points will be explained + activities (including making posters, treasure hunts, simulated entrepreneurship, etc.) to let students understand economic decision-making, economic laws, and the practice and application of economics in daily life.


The curriculum vision
allows children to understand the way of thinking in economics through games, activities and stories, learn to be "little economists" in life, dare to think, calculate, and judge, so as to cultivate future-oriented rational thinking and cooperation skills.


Why study economics?
Primary school students often ask:

  • "Why are some things expensive and some cheap?"

  • "Why do stores discount when I buy something?"

  • "If I have 10 yuan, should I buy a pen or a toy?"

  • "Why are the snacks my classmates want to buy suddenly sold out?" These questions are actually the daily version of economics!


Course Highlights:

  • Life-based explanation: Use real examples such as snacks, toys, and small shops to make concepts such as supply and demand, price, and choice within reach.

  • Interactive activities: make posters, scavenger hunts, and simulate entrepreneurship, so that students can learn to analyze and make decisions through hands-on experience.

  • Teamwork: Complete the "entrepreneurial challenge" in groups and experience the whole process of market operation from idea to "opening".

  • Outcome output: At the end of the course, each student takes away their own economic decision poster or entrepreneurial plan.


Expected harvest

  • Learn to look at life with an "economic perspective"

  • Make it a habit to compare and analyze when choosing

  • Improve communication and teamwork skills

  • Develop a mindset that solves practical problems



Course Structure:

Part 1 Basic Economic Issues (2 hours)


Lesson 1: Limited resources vs. unlimited needs

-What you will learn:

- What is a resource?

- Resources are limited

-Our needs and wants are limitless

-Because resources are limited, we have to make choices

- Give examples of economic goods and free goods so that students can discover the difference

- Teaching method:

- Categorize needs and wants with picture cards (food, water, clothes, mobile phone, amusement park).

-Storytelling: If you have 5 yuan, choose between snacks, stationery, and toys

- Activities:

- Issued to each set of "resource cards" and "list of requirements", asking to choose which needs to be met among the limited cards

- Discussion: Why can't you satisfy everything? How did you choose?

or


Treasure hunt classification competition

Duration: 25 minutes

Materials: 12 cards hidden in advance on campus or in the classroom (6 "economic goods" such as pencils, 6 "free goods" such as sunshine icons)

Steps:

(1) A team of 4 people will set off on a treasure hunt, with a time limit of 10 minutes.

(2) Come back and paste the card on the two columns of the big poster: Economic / Free.

(3) Discussion: Why is air and sunlight free? Why do pencils cost money?

Teams that are correctly classified and can give new examples are rewarded (snacks)


 

Lesson 2: Production and Selection

- Learning content

- Understand the four elements of production: land, capital, labor, enterprise

- Understand that changing these elements affects the quantity or quality of product production

- Opportunity cost definition: the second most important thing that must be given up in order to get something

-Understand that "you can't have both fish and bear's paws"

- Examples in everyday life

- Activities:

The group opened a "milk tea shop" and divided the roles

Land: Draw a booth design

Labor: Recruiting people

Capital: List the tools to be purchased (juicer, cup)

Enterprise: Elect a CEO and come up with a brand name + advertising slogan

The output is presented in the form of posters

or


- "Time budget" game: each person has only 4 hours of free time a day, and it is necessary to arrange an activity schedule (games, sports, drawing, reading...). ）

- Share your options and opportunity costs



Part 2 Market and Supply and Demand (3 hours)


Lesson 3: Buyers and sellers

- Learning content

- What is the market

- Buyers and sellers meet in the market

- The role of the market: to allow resources to flow to those who need it most

- Give examples of microeconomic and macroeconomic decisions in the market

- Teaching methods

-Make "goods" with fruits, stationery, toys

- Teach simple "price" concepts

-activity

- Stall game: divided into buyer groups and seller groups, the price will change in each round, depending on how resources are allocated

- Record and discuss: Why do some things sell fast? How do price changes affect buyer sellers?

 

Lesson 4: Demand and supply

- Learning content

- When something is popular, the price rises

-When there are many things and no one buys them, the price will drop

- Teaching methods

-Experiment with popcorn: At first there was little popcorn → students snapped up → prices went up

-Put a lot more popcorn → the price drops

-activity

- Small group simulations of the price changes of ice cream sold in summer and winter

 

Lesson 5: Price changes

- Learning content

- When demand or supply changes, the price will be automatically adjusted

- High price → Sellers are willing to produce more, buyers buy less

-Low price → Sellers produce less and buyers buy more

- This adjustment helps to allocate resources to the right places

- Teaching methods

- Review with the results of previous stall games: how price changes affect buyers and sellers

-Tell the story of "strawberry season": when there are more strawberries, the price becomes cheaper, and everyone buys more

-activity

- Market Change Challenge: Students are given different scenarios (shortage of goods, sudden popularity, oversupply) and asked them to adjust prices as buyers and sellers

- Discussion: What examples of price changes have you seen in your life?

 


Part 3 Microeconomics (3 to 4 hours)


Lesson 6: Where Does the Money Come From? Where to go?

  • What you will learn: Understand the form and function of money (buying, saving, calculating value), the role of banks (central banks and commercial banks), and how households decide to spend/save/borrow money


Learn about family financial decisions, including:

  • How to spend money when income is high vs. when income is low

  • The meaning of saving money

  • Interest is paid on borrowing money


Lesson 7: Why do people work?

  • Learning content: Understand the factors that affect people's choice of work and being chosen by the company, how wages are determined, why wages are different, and the meaning of the division of labor (efficiency), and feel the impact and advantages and disadvantages of the "division of labor" through activities.


  • Activity: Factory Games can be used to simulate the division of labor

  • Divided into two groups: one group divided the labor (such as biscuits step-by-step, cutting, spreading, and packaging), and the other group completed it independently and then added it up.

  • Make a "cookie" simulation with a piece of paper.

  • Compare which group produces more and faster, discuss student feelings (repetitive division of labor, boredom, etc.).




Lesson 8 & 9 How did the company grow?

  • What you will learn: Understand the types of companies (by size or industry type), the advantages and disadvantages of small and large companies, and how the company grows (expand by itself (open more branches), merge (horizontal/vertical/mixed)). and the simple concept of cost and revenue, understanding the disadvantages of monopoly and the advantages and disadvantages of market competition.

Noun:

Understand economies of scale: Large companies have lower costs due to bulk procurement, mechanization, etc., but they may also be less efficient (not economies of scale).

 Understand production vs. productivity: production vs. per person.

 Understanding Costs and Revenue:

  • Cost: Money spent (buying raw materials, paying wages)

  • Income: The money you make from selling things

  • Profit = Revenue - Cost


  • Activity: Simulated market and monopoly

*Students are divided into merchants and customers

Round 1: Free competition market

    • Each merchant sets up a stall to sell similar goods (such as candy), but can set their own prices or offer additional services (such as giving one more and packing it well).

    • Customers can shop, compare prices, and buy at will.

Discussion:

    • Which one sells the most? Why?

    • Is the price high or low?

    • How do customers feel?

Round 2: Monopoly market

    • Only one merchant is allowed to sell (e.g., a teacher designates a group as the only seller).

    • This merchant can set prices as much as you want, with no competitors.

    • Customers have to buy from this one, otherwise they can't buy anything.

    • Discuss market changes.
